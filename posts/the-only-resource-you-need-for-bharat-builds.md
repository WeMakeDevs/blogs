Building at a hackathon needs three different things at three different stages:

- Something to learn from before the clock starts
- Somewhere to build once it does
- Somewhere for the work to live after the hackathon period ends

Most students use a separate tool for each one. The courses sit in one place, the project sits in a repository, and the two never meet. An hour spent learning leaves no record anywhere, practice stops wherever setup or credits get in the way, and once the hackathon is over there is nothing a reviewer or a recruiter can open that shows what was built and what was understood.

AWS Builder Center holds all three under one free profile. This article covers all the benefits of AWS Builder Center and what you are missing out on if you do not have a profile there yet.

There is a hackathon to test it against right now. [First Commit](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/first-commit), the opening hackathon of the [Bharat Builds Tour](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws) with AWS, runs from 17 to 20 September 2026, online across India, with an optional [in-person build day](https://luma.com/first-commit) in Bangalore on 19 September. A verified Builder Center profile is required to compete, and it carries $100 in AWS credits per participant, which makes setting one up the first task of the weekend rather than an optional extra.

## Start With a Profile & Not a Course
![builder-center-profile](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/2.png)

Signing in uses an AWS Builder ID. It is free, it needs only an email address, and it is separate from an AWS account, so creating one commits you to nothing.

The profile it creates is public and sits at a readable address in the form builder.aws.com/community/@youralias, which makes it a link you can put on a CV or in an application. It holds your bio and social links, the articles you publish, the badges you earn and the communities you join.

Two things are worth doing straight away: write the About section and upload a photo. Both take a minute, both are the first things a visitor sees, and both earn badges that count towards Student Rewards.

## Learn By Building & Not Just By Watching

Everything on Builder Center carries a level, from 100 to 400. A 100 assumes no prior knowledge of the service, while a 400 assumes you have already run it in production. That level sits on every workshop and article, so you know what you are opening before you commit an afternoon to it.

### Workshops: Built by AWS Engineers
Workshops are the most useful part of Builder Center for a hackathon. There are hundreds of them, each a step-by-step lab that has you build something rather than watch someone explain it.

The catalogue is large enough that filtering is the real skill, and you can narrow it down by any of the following:

- Category: such as machine learning, security or serverless
- AWS service: such as Lambda, S3, EC2 or Bedrock
- Level: from 100 through to 400
- Language: since many workshops are published in more than one
- Duration: from a single hour up to eight
- Access type: including which workshops come with a free sandbox attached

Picking one that matches the service your project needs is a far better use of the days before a hackathon than watching another overview video.

![builder-center-workshops](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/3.png)

*Filtering by level, language and service narrows hundreds of workshops to the two that fit.*

### Sandboxes: Eight Hours at a Time

Sandboxes are pre-provisioned AWS environments attached to eligible workshops. Each one gives eight hours of access from the moment you activate it, cleans itself up afterwards, and most become available within about fifteen minutes. You can request one per week, and the allowance resets every Sunday. That weekly limit is worth planning around, because a sandbox spent carelessly on Tuesday is gone until the following week.

### The Toolbox & The Playgrounds

![image](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/5.png)

The toolbox holds SDKs, open source projects and project scaffolding, and the capabilities explorer lets you compare AWS services side by side when you are deciding which one a step in your project needs. The AI playgrounds are there for experimenting with generative AI without wiring up an application first.

## Publish What You Built

This is the part most students skip, and it is the part that keeps returning value long after the weekend.

Builder Center lets you publish articles directly to your profile. A write-up of what you built, which service did what, what broke and what you would do differently reads far better than a repository with three commits and no README. It is also searchable, which a private repository is not.

First Commit hackathon rewards this explicitly, with gaming keyboards going to the five best project write-ups. That is a small prize attached to a habit worth keeping for reasons that have nothing to do with the prize.

![builder-center-profile](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/7.png)


*A profile carries the bio, the links and the badges, earned ones in colour.*

Badges accumulate on the profile as you take part, and they map to real actions rather than to attendance. Publishing your first article earns one. Commenting for the first time earns another. Reading ten articles, publishing your first wish, getting ten votes on your wishes and getting replies on ten of your comments each earn one more.

## Verify Your Student Status, Unlock $579 in Rewards

![builder-center-student-rewards](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/8.png)

*The Student Rewards page, where verification starts.*

Student Rewards is a separate programme on Builder Center and it is worth setting up properly, because the headline benefit arrives immediately.

AWS describes the setup as three steps and five minutes. Verification runs through SheerID and asks for your name, college name and school email address, it is open to students aged eighteen or over enrolled at accredited higher education institutions, and it operates in more than sixty countries. Most verifications complete within minutes, and the ones that need extra documentation usually take a day or two, which is the reason to do this before a hackathon weekend rather than during it. 

Once you are verified and your profile is complete, you get twelve months of AWS Skill Builder Premium, which covers more than nine hundred courses, hands-on labs, certification exam preparation and game-based learning. AWS puts the value of that at $449.

The rest of the programme unlocks through badges, of which there are twenty-one in total.

![student-rewards-ladder](images/the-only-resource-you-need-for-bharat-builds/9.png)

*What verification pays immediately, and what the badges add on top.*

Being straight about this matters more than selling it. The first ten badges are genuinely quick, because they come from filling in your About section, adding a photo, commenting, reading ten articles, publishing your first wish and your first article, and similar one-off actions. 

The remaining eleven are streaks and cumulative milestones, including seven-day, thirty-day and ninety-day runs of visiting, commenting and liking, plus publishing an article every week for four weeks. Nobody reaches twenty-one badges over a hackathon weekend. Reaching them is a habit built over a few months, and the certification voucher at the end is the reward for that habit rather than for a sprint.

## Networking With Resources

**Spaces** are groups built around topics, use cases and communities. They come in public form, which any signed-in builder can join and read, private form, which needs approval from an owner or administrator, and invite-only form, which does not appear in discovery at all. Inside a space you can post text and images, comment, react and search past discussions, and the platform supports sixteen languages.

**User groups and Student Builder Groups** connect you to builders near you. Student Builder Groups are student-led communities based at higher education institutions, and Builder Center lets you either join one or start one, which is the more interesting option if nothing exists at your college yet.

**Following** other builders keeps their work in front of you, which is how most people end up learning what to build next rather than being told.

**The Wishlist** is the part students underuse. You can publish a feature request, vote on other people's, and AWS product teams read and respond to them. Very few places let a second-year student put a request in front of the team that owns the service, and doing it once is also a badge.
