- **The data catalog market is compounding**: Independent market studies that cover the global data catalog segment often place it around USD 1.5 billion in the mid-2020s and project compound annual growth rates frequently quoted between about 20% and 30% into the early 2030s, with several published forecasts pointing to a multi-billion dollar category by that horizon.

- **AI makes weak metadata expensive**: Retrieval, copilots, and agents amplify whatever context you feed them. When column definitions, lineage, and policy tags are wrong or stale, model output looks confident and is still wrong.

If those trends match what your leadership deck already claims, this hackathon is a hands-on way to build where the spend and attention are going.

## Why OpenMetadata And Why Now?

![Split illustration contrasting one engineer drowning in data chaos with a team working calmly around a single Metadata Central hub connected to databases, analytics, and governance](images/back-to-metadata/why-openmetadata.jpeg)

Most teams add new tables, jobs, and dashboards faster than anyone can write the story of how they connect. That gap is how two charts can show different answers for the same metric, or how a new engineer spends a week learning which pipeline actually feeds production.

[OpenMetadata](https://open-metadata.org/) is an open platform that keeps discovery, observability, and governance in one place so people can look up assets, follow lineage, and apply policies from a single source of truth instead of scattered notes and tribal knowledge.

The platform is open source, developed in public with community contributions, while Collate backs hosted and enterprise deployments when teams want official support. Connectors pull metadata from databases, warehouses, and BI tools into one catalog, where lineage, data quality, and governance sit next to full API access, so people can trace data, apply policies, and integrate other systems without maintaining a separate shadow catalog.

## Choose Your Timeline: The Six Paradoxes

We grouped project ideas into six tracks so you are not staring at a blank page. Think of them as alternate timelines you can repair, not boxes you must stay inside.

![A hackathon participant standing at a START platform where six colored arrows branch off in different directions under a banner reading Choose Your Timeline](images/back-to-metadata/timeline.jpeg)

1. **MCP Ecosystem and AI Agents**: If you want to explore how models and tools talk to metadata in natural language, auto-classification, smarter discovery, or assistants that know your catalog better than your newest hire, this is your lane.

2. **Data Observability**: Dashboards, alerts, anomaly hooks, anything that makes "something looks wrong" visible before it becomes someone panicking on Slack.

3. **Connectors and Ingestion**: New sources, better ingestion paths, ETL-shaped glue, anything that gets reality into the catalog without manual babysitting.

4. **Developer Tooling and CI/CD**: CLIs, GitHub Actions, IDE affordances, the boring infrastructure that makes the right thing the easy thing.

5. **Community and Comms**: Bots, notifications, lightweight dashboards that keep humans aligned when metadata changes.

6. **Governance and Classification**: Tagging, PII workflows, policy-shaped automation, the work that stops your future self from apologizing to legal.

You can browse a fuller [idea board on GitHub](https://github.com/orgs/open-metadata/projects/107/views/1) from the hackathon page if you want prompts that are more specific than "build something cool".

## Your Roadmap To Winning

![A team of four developers celebrating around a table with a trophy, laptop, tablet, keyboard, and a bag of prize money](images/back-to-metadata/roadmap-to-winning.jpeg)

These four moves line up with how judges think about submissions. Treat them as your plan, not a vague wish list.

1. **Start from a real problem**: Something in the data ecosystem that you can name in one sentence. Vague "nice to have" ideas rarely survive scoring.

2. **Push past the first idea**: Creativity and innovation are weighted heavily, so the obvious path is rarely the winning path.

3. **Put OpenMetadata at the center**: Deep integration with APIs, ingestion, lineage, or governance beats a thin wrapper that barely touches the platform.

4. **Tell the story**: A clear README plus a demo that shows the problem before the fix often decides placement when scores are close.

Check all four boxes on the build track and you are in the running for the $7,000 prize pool.
