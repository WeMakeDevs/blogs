Between Diwali and New Year, India does more of everything. More shopping, more travelling, more weddings, more gifting, more going home. Almost all of it runs on software nobody built for the job: a family WhatsApp group with four hundred unread messages, a spreadsheet somebody's cousin maintains, a notes app listing who sent what, and six browser tabs open on a train booking page at 9:59 in the morning.

We patch our way through those weeks every year and then forget about it until the next one. Festive Hacks is us asking you to fix one of them properly.

It is on Wednesday 25 November at BIEC in Bengaluru, in person, on the floor of WeAreDevelopers Conference India. Hacking runs from nine in the morning to five in the evening, and the winners are called up on stage at six. Seats on the floor are limited, so you apply rather than register, and that is the one thing worth doing before you finish reading this.

![The schedule for 25 November at BIEC Bengaluru](images/festive-hacks-one-day-in-bengaluru/the-day.png)

## Getting in

Applications are at **[luma.com/festivehacks](https://luma.com/festivehacks)**. It asks a few basic questions, we read every one of them ourselves, and we pick who hacks with us.

If we accept you, your conference pass for both days is free, and that pass is a ₹10,000 ticket. Travel and where you stay are yours to sort out, so please only apply if you can actually be in Bengaluru on the 25th.

You can hack alone or in a team of up to four. Every single person on the team has to apply and be accepted, so do not assume your friends are in because you are. That one catches people out every time.

## What the winning team gets

The winning team flies to Berlin for WeAreDevelopers World Congress in July 2027. Flights, stay, passes and expenses are covered, for the whole team, not one person from it. World Congress filled Messe Berlin last year. A funded trip there with the people you built it with is not something that comes around often.

Second place is the best use of Flagsmith, and takes ₹1 lakh in cash, Keychron keyboards for everyone on the team and $1,000 in Sarvam AI credits. Third place takes the keyboards and the Sarvam AI credits.

## What to build, and what to build it with

Pick one real problem from the festive weeks, build something that solves it, and show it working. Narrow beats broad here. A tool that tracks which wedding vendor has been paid is a better project than a general-purpose event platform, because you can finish the first one in a day and put it in front of someone.

There are no tracks. To win, your project has to use all three sponsor tools, and they are not there to tick a box, because each one covers a different part of getting something working in a day.

**[Flagsmith](https://www.flagsmith.com/)** is open source feature flags and experimentation. The versions of a thing sit behind a flag, you turn them on without a redeploy, roll them out to the right people and A/B test what you ship. If you want to see the whole loop before the 25th, we wrote one up: [how to run a real A/B test with Flagsmith Experimentation](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-run-a-real-ab-test-with-flagsmith-experimentation).

**[NudgeBee](https://nudgebee.com/)** is an open source agentic ops platform. Its agents look into incidents, cut cloud and Kubernetes waste, and run approval-gated runbooks across your infrastructure. It is the sort of thing people leave out of a hackathon project and then have nothing to show when a judge asks how it would survive contact with real traffic.

**[Sarvam AI](https://www.sarvam.ai/)** is AI models built for India. Speech to text, text to speech, translation and chat across 22 Indian languages and English, through one API. For anything festive, that is often what decides whether your family can use it or only you can.

Get signed up for all three this week. Keys, first call, done, so that none of it happens on the morning of the 25th.

## If you need a direction

Six places we keep coming back to. **Wedding season** runs November to February, which means hundreds of guests, a dozen vendors and a family group chat holding the whole thing together, so RSVPs, vendor tracking and a shagun ledger all live here. **Gifting** is Diwali hampers, Secret Santa and the client gift list. **Going home** is Tatkal at 10 AM, the sold-out bus and the cab split four ways. **Year in review** is that everyone wants a Wrapped by December and nothing outside music gives them one. **Get-togethers** is the office party and the potluck nobody coordinated. **Money in December** is a bonus, a sale season and a January bill all arriving at once.

None of that is a requirement. It is there so nobody spends the first hour of the day staring at an empty editor.

Whatever you pick, it has to run rather than live in slides. Submissions need a public repo, a README someone else can set it up from, and a demo video, all filed by 5 PM.

## How the day runs

Check-in opens at 8 AM at BIEC, so bring your laptop and your charger. Hacking starts on the conference floor at 9 and ends at 5, which is when submissions close. Winners go up on stage at 6.

Hitesh Choudhary and Saiyam Pathak are judging. Around you, over the two days, the conference has Thomas Dohmke, Wolfgang Platz, Hadar Geva, Jack Fitzsimons, Christian Heilmann and Amol Sharma on stage, along with more than a hundred other speakers and three thousand people. Your pass covers the 26th as well, and the hallway on day two is worth the trip on its own.

Use the code **WEMAKEDEVS999** at [tickets.sarvarth.com](https://tickets.sarvarth.com) and a two-day pass is ₹999 instead of ₹10,000. There are only so many at that price. Same hall, same speakers, and you will be in the room when the Berlin trip gets handed over.

## What to do this week

Today, apply on Luma. Seats are limited, we read applications as they arrive, and nothing else here matters if you are not in the room.

Tomorrow and the day after, sign up for Flagsmith, NudgeBee and Sarvam AI and make one call against each. Ten minutes a tool, and the 25th then starts with your idea instead of a signup form.

Over the weekend, pick the problem. Go back through the festive weeks you have already lived through and write down the three moments where you did something by hand that a computer should have handled. One of those is your project. Walking in already knowing which one is worth more than anything you can code the night before.

Then sort out your team, if you want one. Two people who have built together before usually get further in eight hours than four who met at check-in.

Apply at **[luma.com/festivehacks](https://luma.com/festivehacks)**, and everything else is on the [Festive Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/hackathons/festivehacks).

See you at BIEC on the 25th.
